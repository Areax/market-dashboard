Statistical details Firings on the same day are averaged first, because correlated stocks moving together aren't independent bets. Each run's t-stat uses a Newey-West-style correction for overlapping holding periods. Placebo runs shift all of a signal's firing dates together by every allowed number of trading days, wrapping around the window, which keeps the real bunching of firings. Shifts close to the signal's own setup period are skipped (60 days, or 260 for the trend template and 52-week breakout), because a placebo landing on the run-up a signal is built from would look unfairly good. The luck score is the placebo p-value converted to a z-score. Costs are a flat {{ '%.1f' % (study.cost * 100) }}% round trip; short borrow fees and taxes aren't modeled. A second share class (e.g. GOOG next to GOOGL) is left out of the study so one company isn't counted twice. Prices are dividend-adjusted, so 52-week highs and pattern levels can differ slightly from what a chart showed at the time.

Reading the event study

Net vs SPY
What trading the signal earned compared with simply holding SPY over the same days, after a {{ '%.1f' % ((study.cost if study else 0.002) * 100) }}% round-trip cost. Firings on the same day are averaged together first.
Edge (pre-cost)
Net vs SPY with what every stock here did on those same days and what that particular stock usually did taken out, then compared with the same signal on random other dates. What's left is the signal's timing: 0 = no better than luck. It's before costs.
Luck score
How far the real result lands outside the placebo runs (the same signal on random other dates). Near 0 = indistinguishable from luck; 3 or more (or −3 or less) = beat (or lost to) almost every placebo run. With this many tests, scores around 2 still show up by chance now and then. Because both are measured against the placebo, a tiny edge and a tiny score can occasionally point in different directions; both mean "nothing there".
Win rate
The share of individual firings that came out ahead after costs.

Holdings columns

Weight
The stock's share of the sector ETF.
Trend
✓ when every condition of the trend template (above) holds today; – when any one doesn't. Most stocks are – most of the time, and that's normal.
RS (relative strength)
Where the stock's 12-month return ranks among these companies, 0-100. 80 means it beat 80% of them.
From high
How far the price is below its highest point of the past year.
10-ATR stop
Today's price minus 10 times the average daily price range over the last 14 days (ATR). It's the starting stop-loss level used in the breakout research. Blank for very volatile stocks, where it would fall to zero or below.
Moving averages
"10-month avg" / "200-day avg" are the plain average closing price over that span. The 21-day line in your candle signals is an exponential average, which weights recent days more.

All signals use completed trading days only, never the day still in progress. A chart-pattern turning point counts only once 5 more days confirm it. Prices are adjusted for splits and dividends. Data: Yahoo Finance.
